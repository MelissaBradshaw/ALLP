about Lowell and project: ex: Amy Lowell wrote and received thousands of letters — to editors, publishers, poets, and rivals — carbon copies of which she kept meticulously, typed on her own machine. This archive treats each letter not as an isolated document but as one strand in a wider correspondence network radiating outward from Lowell herself.

What we want readers to be aware of, e.g. annotations, strikethrough, corrections, etc.

Sevenels · 70 Heath Street · Brookline, Massachusetts
April 3, 1916

Dear Miss Monroe,

I return herewith the unrhymed cadences you asked after, though I confess I am furious I remain unconvinced that Poetry has given the Imagists their due this quarter.

You will recall that Mr. Pound and I no longer see eye to eye on the matter of the anthology, and I should like this carbon kept on file so there can be no confusion as to what passed between us.

— A.L.

Editorial / pedagogical note

Struck-through words, corrections, and marginalia record a moment of composition, not just its final form. Students using this archive are invited to compare carbons against recipients' originals where both survive, and to ask: whose voice does an archive preserve, and whose does it silence?
